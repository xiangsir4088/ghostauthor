# Commercial model

> This document is the business design behind `ghostauthor`. It is written
> for the maintainer first (so pricing and scope decisions have a written
> rationale) and for a prospective buyer second (so the boundary between the
> free tool and the paid surface is not a surprise).

## 1. What is actually being sold

Not the CLI. The CLI is MIT, installable in one command, and complete for a
single repository. Selling it would be selling something the buyer can
`pip install` in ten seconds.

What is being sold is **a number that survives cross-examination**.

Every organisation now reports an "AI code percentage". Almost none can
defend it. The number comes from token usage (measures activity), a style
classifier (measures resemblance), or a vendor dashboard (measures whatever
that vendor can see). When an auditor, a board, an insurer or an acquirer
asks *"which commits, which agent, when, and can you reproduce that?"*, none
of those sources answers.

`ghostauthor` answers it, because it reads signatures that were written at
commit time rather than inferring intent after the fact. The commercial
product is therefore not "AI detection". It is:

> **Evidence of AI provenance that a third party can re-run and get the same
> bytes.**

That is a narrow claim, and narrow claims are what audits are made of.

## 2. Who buys, and what triggers the purchase

Three segments, ordered by how urgently they feel the problem.

### A. Regulated engineering organisations (primary)

Banks, insurers, healthcare, defence suppliers, listed companies under
disclosure obligations. The trigger is external and dated: an audit finding,
an ISO/IEC 42001 gap assessment, an EU AI Act transparency question, a
customer security questionnaire that now asks "how is AI-generated code
tracked?".

They do not want a dashboard. They want a **document they can attach to an
audit response**, plus a person who can explain the method. This is why the
HTML brief and the evidence bundle exist, and why the report carries no
timestamp: an auditor's first question about a generated document is "was
this edited after the fact?".

Budget holder: CISO, Head of Engineering Governance, or the compliance
function. Typical deal: annual, low five figures.

### B. Engineering organisations selling to enterprises (secondary)

SaaS vendors and consultancies answering due-diligence questionnaires. The
trigger is a deal in progress: a customer's procurement team asks for AI
usage disclosure, and the vendor has nothing but an estimate.

They need a **repeatable answer per engagement**, not a governance programme.
Typical deal: mid four figures annually, or per-report.

### C. Individual maintainers and small teams (funnel, not revenue)

They will never pay for this, and that is fine — they are distribution.
Every one of them who runs `ghostauthor` in CI, links a report in a README,
or installs the skill is a reference the primary segments can find. The free
tier is deliberately not crippled: a crippled free tier produces no
references.

## 3. The cold-start problem is the commercial problem

The single largest cause of churn is not a missing feature. It is this:

```
$ ghostauthor ./repo
commits: 1204 total
  agent-signed        0  (0.0%)
  cosigned-unknown    3  (0.2%)
  unsigned         1201  (99.8%)
```

A correct report that looks like a broken tool. The operator concludes the
product does not work and never comes back. This is why `coverage` exists and
why it is the first command in the README: it turns a zero into a diagnosis
with two different, concrete fixes.

Any commercial packaging that does not solve this first is selling into a
funnel with a hole in the bottom.

## 4. Product boundary

The line is drawn where work stops being a command and becomes a deliverable.

| Capability | Free (MIT) | Paid |
|---|---|---|
| Audit, JSON / text / markdown / SARIF | yes | — |
| `coverage` diagnosis, `portfolio` over local checkouts | yes | — |
| Evidence per commit, reproducible bundle | yes | — |
| Signing (`sign`, `install-hook`), MCP server, local web UI | yes | — |
| HTML brief, unbranded | yes | — |
| HTML brief, branded / white-label, engagement metadata | — | yes |
| Scheduled scans, retained snapshots, trend lines | — | yes |
| Policy-as-code: per-path, per-team, per-agent rules | — | yes |
| Organisation roll-up across hundreds of repos, SSO, retention | — | yes |
| Methodology pack + reviewer Q&A for an audit | — | yes |

Two rules keep this honest:

1. **Nothing moves from free to paid later.** A capability listed as free
   stays free. The paid surface is new work, not a fence around old work.
2. **The JSON contract is frozen on both sides.** A report generated by the
   free CLI today validates against the same schema in the paid product
   next year. Lock-in through data format is not part of the model.

## 5. Pricing

Priced by **repositories under management**, not by seat. Developers do not
want to buy a seat for an audit tool, and per-seat pricing would penalise
exactly the broad adoption the ledger needs to be meaningful.

| Tier | Price | Includes |
|---|---|---|
| Free | $0 | Everything in this repository, unlimited local use |
| Team | $490 / month, annual | 25 repositories, scheduled scans, 12-month history, branded briefs, policy-as-code |
| Enterprise | from $18,000 / year | Unlimited repositories, on-prem or private cloud, SSO, retention policy, methodology pack, named reviewer support |
| Engagement | from $6,000 / report | Due-diligence or audit-support report on a defined scope, delivered as a signed evidence bundle |

Rationale for the shape, not the digits:

- **Annual, not monthly-only.** The value is a retained history; a monthly
  plan invites a monthly cancellation after the audit passes.
- **Repository-counted.** It maps to how the buyer already budgets
  (repos are the unit of platform spend) and it grows with the account.
- **The engagement tier exists because it is the fastest path to the first
  dollar** and the best source of product requirements.

Digits are hypotheses. They should be re-set after five customer
conversations, not defended.

## 6. Why open-core and not SaaS-first

A hosted product would be the natural shape for this category, and it is the
wrong first move here:

- The buyer's strongest objection is *"how do I know your number is right?"*
  A local, reproducible, MIT tool answers it before the question is finished.
  A hosted black box does the opposite.
- The data is sensitive (commit messages, author identities). On-prem is a
  feature, not a compromise, for segment A.
- A solo maintainer cannot carry the availability, security review and
  support load of a multi-tenant service while employed elsewhere.

So: **open-core now, hosted later only if the paid tiers prove demand.**

## 7. Distribution

Ordered by cost-effectiveness for a solo maintainer.

1. **PyPI + the repository.** `pip install ghostauthor` is the conversion
   event. Everything else points at it.
2. **MCP directories and agent skill marketplaces.** The MCP server is a
   distribution channel: an agent that can call `repo_authorship_report` is
   an agent that surfaces the tool inside someone's editor. The packaged
   skill (`ghostauthor-ai-code-audit/`) exists for this.
3. **The compliance content angle.** The defensible narrative — *"your AI
   code percentage is not auditable, and here is why"* — is a better
   acquisition asset than any feature list. It is also the article a CISO
   forwards internally.
4. **Direct outreach to segment B.** Due-diligence questionnaires are
   recurring, dated and public enough to find. A single free report on a
   prospect's public repository is a stronger opening than an email.

## 8. Competition and moat

| Category | Examples | Their number comes from | Weakness this exploits |
|---|---|---|---|
| AI code governance platforms | Arnica, diwo, etc. | Scanning + policy + dashboards | Attribution is inferred, not signed; the evidence layer is the soft spot |
| Host-bound provenance | GitLab `refs/notes/ai` | Host metadata | Only works if the whole org lives on that host |
| Style detectors | generic AI-text classifiers | Probability | Cannot name a commit; not admissible |
| Usage analytics | token dashboards | Session activity | Measures activity, not output |

The moat is not the code — it is small and readable on purpose. The moat is:

1. **The contract discipline.** Frozen schema, verify matrix, byte-stable
   output. A competitor can copy the feature in a week; copying the
   discipline that makes the number admissible takes a year.
2. **The roster as data.** Every agent added to `agents.json` by the
   community widens coverage without a code change. This is a compounding
   asset that a closed product cannot build as fast.
3. **The refusal to guess.** "Unsigned ≠ human" is a positioning that
   disqualifies most competitors from the audit use case, because their
   product's core claim is the guess.

## 9. Risks

| Risk | Severity | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Agents stop writing `Co-Authored-By` trailers, or write a different standard | High | Roster is data; add a parser per convention. The `coverage` command makes the gap visible before it is fatal |
| A host ships native provenance and bundles it free | Medium | Stay host-agnostic; be the tool that reads *all* hosts into one ledger |
| Buyers treat "AI code %" as a nice-to-have until a regulation bites | Medium | Sell the engagement (dated, budgeted) before the platform |
| Solo-maintainer support load | High | Cap Enterprise accounts; make the methodology pack do the explaining |
| The number is gamed (agents sign commits they did not write) | Medium | Documented in `docs/attestation.md`: the tool records claims, humans verify them. Never sell it as fraud detection |

## 10. Twelve-month plan

| Quarter | Focus | Exit criterion |
|---|---|---|
| Q1 | Product surface: `coverage`, HTML brief, `portfolio` (v1.5) | A stranger can go from `pip install` to a board-ready brief in ten minutes |
| Q2 | Policy-as-code + trend snapshots (v1.6) | A team can express "no path under `payments/` above 40%" and have CI enforce it |
| Q3 | First three paid engagements, run manually | Three signed reports, three written testimonials, a priced methodology pack |
| Q4 | Automate what the engagements repeated | Hosted scheduled scans for the retainers that renewed |

The sequencing rule: **sell the manual version before automating it.** Every
hour spent on a dashboard before the first paid report is a guess about what
the dashboard should show.

## 11. Honest revenue expectation

Year one, realistic: **$0–$15,000**. Not because the product is weak, but
because trust products sell slowly, the buyer is an institution, and the
maintainer has a day job.

The return that is not revenue:

- A public, defensible artefact for an SRE/AI-operations career — the tool
  demonstrates exactly the discipline that governance roles are hired for.
- A direct line into the compliance conversation at target employers.
- A distribution asset (PyPI installs, MCP directory listing) that compounds.

Anyone projecting six figures in year one for a solo, open-core audit tool is
selling a story. This document is not that story.

## 12. Objection handling

**"We already have a dashboard from our AI vendor."**
Ask what it reports when the agent is Copilot, Cursor and Claude Code in the
same repository, on different days. Vendor dashboards are per-vendor by
construction; a ledger reads the repository.

**"Our developers will not adopt a signing convention."**
They already did — the agents write the trailer by default in most cases.
`coverage` measures how true that is in *your* repository before you commit
to anything.

**"Can't someone just forge a trailer?"**
Yes. The tool records claims and makes them auditable; it does not detect
fraud, and `docs/attestation.md` says so in the product. An audit tool that
claims to detect lying is the one thing an auditor will not trust.

**"Why not just use `git log`?"**
`git log` will show you the trailer. It will not aggregate it, attribute it
against a roster, produce a frozen JSON contract, gate CI on it, or hand you
a reproducible bundle with a sha256 manifest. The gap between "the data is
there" and "the data is evidence" is the product.
